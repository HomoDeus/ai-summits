# Evidence and editorial policy

AI Summits is a curated, community-editable atlas. It mixes research questions, practical challenges and clearly scoped benchmark targets; they are not comparable units of difficulty. The initial 26 entries span 19 editorial discipline groups, not a universal taxonomy or complete census.

## Status

- **Open frontier** (`open`): no AI milestone is recorded on or before the selected year. This is an absence of a catalog entry, not proof that AI has made no contribution or that humans have never made progress.
- **AI breakthrough** (`partial`): a documented advance in a restricted part of the challenge. Includes benchmark results, simulations, experiments, verified constructions and assisted discoveries. It is an editorial grouping, not a universal measure of scientific importance.
- **Target achieved** (`achieved`): the precisely worded target is met by the evidence. Winning a particular Go match qualifies; mathematically solving Go does not follow.

A theorem can only be marked achieved with an accepted proof of the stated theorem. A clinical target cannot be marked achieved from a computational screen or animal study. A social objective cannot be marked achieved from a simulation. Where human input was essential, identify it rather than claiming autonomous AI discovery.

## Milestones and replay

Milestones are dated by the documented event or publication. A publication-year entry must not be presented as the first-ever achievement unless verified. Records may contain multiple milestones; replay selects the latest eligible entry. Problem context, boundaries and sources are current editorial context and do not rewind. The interface explicitly labels this distinction.

The initial collection emphasizes selected 2016–2025 historical milestones; it is not a state-of-the-art tracker. No record promises that its last milestone is the latest known result. Source review dates are stored per record.

## Sources and disputes

Prefer original research and evaluation organizers; use official scientific or public institutions for problem context. Institutional announcements can document a reported result but are not independent replication. Source titles identify provenance. For philosophical context, a scholarly reference work is acceptable. All text here is original summary; linked material retains its own rights.

For a disputed result, add the competing primary evidence and explain the uncertainty before raising its status. If an existing milestone is invalidated, correct/remove the invalid status with the evidence in a PR; Git preserves the correction history. The current schema deliberately has no automated credibility score or forecast.

## Map semantics

Numbered peaks link to exact records. Stable catalog numbers are navigation aids, not ranks. Position, proximity and height are illustrative and may rearrange when filtering. Color encodes the recorded status only. There are no fabricated completion percentages, universal difficulty scores or predictions of a conquest date.

## Updating the collection

Contributors may propose missing disciplines, finer subdisciplines or new milestones. Maintainers review factual wording, source relevance, human/AI attribution and translations together. Keep the problem specific enough that readers can explain what happened and what remains unresolved.
